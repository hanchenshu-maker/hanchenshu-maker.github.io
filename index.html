Research

From UCLA's year-long Data, Justice, and Society cluster, 2025 – 2026. Both projects ask who a model leaves out.

  1. Space, Time, and Spatial Injustice

    A spatial regression of pedestrian footfall in New York City

    2025 – 2026 · Python, GeoPandas, PySAL

    Planners often model foot traffic with ordinary least squares, which treats every street as if its neighbors did not exist. I tested that on NYC DOT's Bi-Annual Pedestrian Counts. A model that ignores geography reads a quiet street as one nobody wants, and steers investment toward hubs that are already busy.

  2. Driving in the Dark

    An essay on where autonomous vehicles get tested, and where they don't

    March 2026

    AV makers test where mapping is easy and customers are dense, leaving "data deserts" where the systems are unreliable. Reading California DMV disengagement reports from 2020 to 2025, I found root causes logged too vaguely to audit, and proposed a public-interest data repository overseen by an equity board within a federal body like NHTSA. Presented to the Active Mobility department at Singapore's LTA.
